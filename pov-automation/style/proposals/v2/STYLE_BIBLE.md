# Bald Life POV — permanent illustration bible v2 candidate

Authority: the existing protagonist sheet and the user's documented preference
in the Michelin production's `images/style_audit_rules.txt` for approved scene 1.
This preserves the locally refined, richly equipped outlined cartoon; it does
not revert to the older instruction to flatten every background into sparse shapes.
The immutable library manifest records exact source bytes and review notes.
No production, retry, or generated image may revise this document or its references.
A deliberate artistic change requires a new version and user direction.

## Required drawing system

- Linework: near-black warm contours, continuous clean rounded joins. Foreground
  silhouettes are bold (visually about 2–4 pixels at a 1672-pixel-wide frame),
  inner folds and construction lines about half that weight. These are visual
  guides, not pixel measurement acceptance thresholds. Distant objects retain
  legible contours with fewer internal marks, not photographic blur. No scratchy
  sketch lines, etched hatching, broken painterly edges or outlines on faces only.
- Colour: restrained naturalistic relationships: cream, charcoal, warm tan,
  warm browns, dusty blues and sage greens. Scene-appropriate colours are allowed;
  a modern kitchen need not be brown like a Roman room. Warm skin remains warm
  light tan for the shared protagonist. Supporting skin tones remain distinct.
  Keep colour masses readable; no neon saturation, HDR contrast or global orange
  grading. Night, weather and practical lamps change hues, not drawing technique.
- Shading: a base fill and one principal cel-shadow family, with a small secondary
  occlusion region when necessary. Broad restrained illustrated tonal changes
  and simplified bands on metal are allowed, as in kitchen scene 1. Skin and
  cloth remain predominantly matte. No mirror-scene reflections, plastic skin,
  ray-traced material response, dense specular gradients, painterly blending,
  photographic microtexture or volumetric light shafts. Shadow edges stay clearly
  drawn; subtle ambient softness must never dissolve contours.
- Character construction: rounded oval heads, small solid dark oval eyes without
  detailed irises, small rounded button noses, rounded visible ears, heavy simple
  expressive eyebrows, concise mouth curves. Preserve the existing protagonist
  sheet's identity and warm skin, short thick neck, rounded limbs, hands and shoes.
  Follow the production's fixed core and age/costume layer; do not independently
  redesign body ratios. Perspective and shot distance may change apparent ratios.
  Supporting people have distinct faces, hair and builds within this construction.
  Expressions use eyebrows, eyelids, mouth and pose, not realistic facial anatomy.
  Young age layers must not inherit wrinkles from older expression references.
- Clothing: large readable folds with a few tapered internal lines; simple seam,
  button and costume details. Required period/occupation equipment is drawn in
  this same system. No fabric weave, skin pores or individual hair rendering.
- Environments: coherent cinematic perspective, straight architectural edges,
  rounded props, identifiable equipment and spatially plausible object placement.
  Richly equipped settings are permitted; do not erase equipment merely to reduce
  detail. Group repeated detail into readable shapes. Foliage uses clustered leaf
  masses; masonry uses selected joints; vehicles and mechanisms use simplified
  outlined panels. Foreground, faces, buildings and objects share the same finish.
  Use reduced detail and colour separation for distance, never depth-of-field blur.
- Composition/finish: wide 16:9 illustrated frames, intentional shot variation,
  clear focal hierarchy, clean edges and integrated lighting. Honour full-body,
  close-up and object framing independently. No text unless the shot requires it.
  Cinematic means framing and narrative emphasis, not a different rendering medium.

## Forbidden substitutions

No photorealism, 3D/plastic rendering, oil/watercolour painting, anime eye design,
alternative sitcom cartoon construction, vector-icon minimalism, texture overlays,
film grain, bokeh, photographic food/material close-ups, glossy realistic
backgrounds behind cartoon people, or increasingly detailed rendering on retries.
Attractive images still fail when they use a different drawing system.

## Reference roles and precedence

Global style > isolated video context > individual shot. All reference subjects,
costumes, props, locations and eras are examples only. Never copy them into a new
story. Identity is separate: only main-character scenes receive the protagonist
sheet. Object-only scenes receive a people-free style reference. People scenes
receive a fixed people rendering example, not the last generated frame. All
reviewers also inspect the common library to keep these routes in one style.

## Visual gate

Inspect the actual candidate at full resolution beside canonical references.
Record pass/fail/uncertain separately for linework, colour, shading, character
construction (or reasoned not-applicable), backgrounds, and finish. Record
identity, story/content and composition separately. Check every visible region:
faces/people, clothing, foreground props, architecture, distance, lighting.
Uncertainty is not a pass. Specific observations and reference/image hashes are
required. Existing approval filenames alone do not prove compliance with v1.
Content failures never redefine style. Feedback is local to an attempt; never
rewrite the global bible to excuse a newly generated output.

## Measurable review scale (candidate v2)

Score every image against the same neutral style sheet and this bible, regardless
of topic, scene number or prior outputs. These scores describe visible deviation;
they are never computed from metadata and never replace pixel inspection.

For line weight, character construction, colour-region treatment, cel shading,
material rendering, and environment/background finish, record:

- **0 — matches:** the image follows the canonical technique; scene-specific
  content or lighting changes do not alter the drawing system.
- **1 — small variation:** a local difference is visible but the image still
  reads as the same illustrator and has no medium substitution.
- **2 — drift:** a clear change in line, anatomy, rendering medium, shading,
  texture, material finish, or background technique. Fail the image.
- **uncertain — hold:** inspect again or request a correction; never approve.

At 1672-pixel image width, primary foreground contours should generally measure
about 2–4 pixels and interior construction lines about half that width. Use these
as an observable range, allowing perspective and scaling; do not infer a pass
from a single pixel sample. Shading normally uses one principal cel-shadow family
and at most a small secondary occlusion region. Skin and cloth remain matte;
material bands stay broad and outlined. Scene palettes vary naturally, without
neon saturation, HDR contrast or global grading.

A scene passes only when all six style dimensions score 0 or 1, content and
identity checks pass, and no unrelated subject or era appears. A production also
requires comparative contact-sheet audits at its pilot, each 20-scene boundary,
and its final batch. Any score 2, content leak, uncertain result, missing review,
or failed batch audit blocks further batches and assembly.

## Reference library proposal status

This v2 bible preserves the v1 rendering system and proposes a content-neutral
style sheet in place of narrative-bearing Roman Legion and modern kitchen style
references. The candidate is not active until the user approves the actual sheet
and its comparison with the existing v1 artwork. The protagonist reference is
byte-for-byte the v1 identity sheet and remains a separate identity reference.
